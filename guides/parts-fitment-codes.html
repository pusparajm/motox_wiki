Motorcycle Parts & Fitment Codes

Spark plugs, brake pads, wheel bearings, steering head bearings and fork seals by model.

Part numbers are not universal. A pad or bearing that fits one model year can miss the next, and ABS, market and mid-year running changes all matter. This page collects the spark plug, brake pad, wheel bearing, steering head bearing and fork seal codes we have been able to verify for individual models.

Where a cell is blank, we have not confirmed a code for that model. A blank is deliberate: it is better than a plausible-looking guess.

For a pad-shape cross-reference across EBC, SBS, Ferodo, Dunlopad, Emgo and Vesrah, see the brake pad cross-reference.

Brake pad and spark plug codes

The table lists the codes shown on each model page. EBC codes come from EBC catalogue listings recorded on MotoSpecVault model-year pages; spark plug codes come from the same model-year records. SBS and Vesrah equivalences come from Delta Braking's shape cross-reference tables. Brembo codes are added only where Brembo's application catalogue confirms the model and year.

Where an SBS entry contains several numbers separated by slashes, those are shape references listed by Delta for the same EBC pad. Pick the compound for your riding and confirm the seller's application list for your exact model year.

Why some models have no bearing or seal code

Bearing and seal fitment is harder to verify than a spark plug code. The same bearing bore can use different widths, seals and spacers, and kit manufacturers revise application lists. We only publish a kit number or seal size when an application list or OEM parts fiche supports it. If your bike is not in the table, measure the old bearing or seal and use the OEM part number as the primary reference.

How to verify a part for your bike

  1. Identify the exact model year, market and ABS/non-ABS variant. “MT-07” alone is not enough.
  2. Look up the OEM part number on a parts fiche for your VIN or model code.
  3. Cross-check the aftermarket application list, not only the product title.
  4. For bearings and seals, measure the old part or read the marking on the bearing race.
  5. For brake pads, compare the pad shape and backing plate; a different compound code can share the same shape.
  6. Use the workshop manual for torque values, fluid volumes and bed-in procedure.
