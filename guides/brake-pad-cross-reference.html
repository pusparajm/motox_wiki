Motorcycle Brake Pad Cross-Reference

EBC, SBS, Ferodo, Dunlopad, Emgo and Vesrah shape equivalents and model fitments.

This page cross-references the brake-pad shapes used across the catalogue. The same backing plate can be sold in several friction compounds, and one shape can fit several models, model years and even several brands. The pad code identifies the shape; the suffix or compound code identifies the material.

Use the tables as a starting point, not as a substitute for the caliper and model-year check. ABS, twin-caliper, market and mid-year running changes can alter the pad. Confirm the seller's application list or the OEM parts fiche before ordering.

Sources: EBC UK Motorcycle Pad Buyers Guide 203, SBS US Brake Pads Catalogue 2023–2024, and the Transcan/EBC pad and shoe interchange list. The Transcan list also covers older and ATV/UTV shapes, so some rows below will not apply to a road motorcycle.

Model fitments in the MotoX catalogue

255 models currently have a verified multi-brand front or rear pad entry. Multiple rows can share the same shape: for example, the same EBC FA630 / SBS 900 front shape appears on several Aprilia, Benelli and BMW models. That does not mean the pads are identical in compound or price. The wider set of SBS-only fitment entries is shown on the individual model pages.

Why one shape fits several models

Brake calipers are often shared across a model family or even across manufacturers. A pad shape is defined by the backing-plate outline, hole spacing, thickness and locating tabs. Once a caliper is used on several bikes, the same shape appears under many model names. The friction compound is separate: a commuter may use an organic or semi-sintered pad, while a sport or race model uses a sintered or race compound in the same shape.

That is why a cross-reference list can show several models from one brand against one code. Within BMW, the F 750 GS and F 850 GS share the SBS 674HS front / 675LS rear pair. Within Honda, the CB500F and CBR500R share the EBC FA760 / SBS 994HS front shape. The compound can still differ by model or model year, so check the suffix and the intended use before mixing a touring pad into a track application or vice versa.
